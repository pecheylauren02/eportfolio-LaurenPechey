Information Security Management

This module introduces the core principles and practices of Information Security Management within organisations. It focuses on understanding how information assets are protected through structured approaches to risk identification, assessment, treatment, and ongoing management. Key themes include security and risk management definitions, core concepts, and the risk management process, as well as an introduction to qualitative and quantitative approaches to assessing risk. The module also explores relevant security and risk standards and how they can be applied in real-world contexts to support effective decision-making and organisational resilience.

UNIT 1

In Unit 1, I was introduced to the fundamental concepts of Information Security and Risk Management. The unit explored common definitions of security and risk, examined key risk management concepts, and provided an overview of the risk management process used to identify, assess, and mitigate risks within organisations.

LEARNING OUTCOMES

  • Discussed the various definitions and interpretations of risk.
  • Explained how risks can be assessed, qualified, and mitigated.
  • Described qualitative and quantitative approaches to risk assessment.
  • Identified common security and risk management standards and determined their suitability for different scenarios.

This unit provided the foundational knowledge required to understand security risk management. It introduced the principles of risk assessment and explored both qualitative and quantitative methods for evaluating risks, which were examined in greater depth throughout the module.

UNIT OVERVIEW AND REFLECTION

In Unit 1, I explored the basic principles of Information Security Management and Risk Management. Through the lecturecast and readings, I developed an understanding of how risk is defined and how it can be assessed using both qualitative and quantitative methods. I also gained insight into how organisations apply structured risk management processes to identify threats, vulnerabilities, and potential impacts.

Reflecting on this unit, I found the distinction between qualitative and quantitative risk assessment particularly useful, as it highlighted the difference between subjective judgement and measurable analysis. Following Rolfe et al. (2001), I considered what I learned, why it is important, and how it can be applied in future studies and professional practice.

This unit provided a clearer understanding of how threat modelling supports risk identification and analysis. It demonstrated how different techniques can be selected or combined depending on system complexity and organisational requirements.

UNIT 4

Security Standards, Frameworks and Disaster Recovery

In Unit 4, I explored key security standards and frameworks used within Information Security Management, as well as their role in risk assessment, security design, and organisational resilience. The unit also introduced disaster recovery concepts and approaches, including Disaster Recovery as a Service (DRaaS), and highlighted how organisations prepare for and respond to major disruptions.

LEARNING OUTCOMES

  • Identified and discussed common security standards and frameworks used in industry.
  • Explained how security standards influence risk assessments and security design decisions.
  • Described disaster recovery concepts and approaches, including DRaaS.
  • Selected appropriate standards and frameworks for different organisational scenarios.

This unit emphasised the importance of aligning security governance with recognised standards to ensure consistency in risk management and compliance. It also highlighted how disaster recovery planning supports business continuity and reduces the impact of security incidents and system failures.

UNIT OVERVIEW AND REFLECTION

In Unit 4, I developed my understanding of security standards, frameworks, and disaster recovery through the lecturecast, readings, and wiki-based activities. The reading provided background on business continuity and disaster recovery planning, including key terminology and evaluation approaches for DR strategies (Alhazmi and Malaiya, 2013). This helped me understand how organisations prepare for and recover from disruptive events.

The wiki activity on security frameworks allowed me to explore how different standards are applied in practice and how they support consistent risk management across organisations. I also gained insight into how these frameworks influence both security design and compliance requirements. The optional GDPR activity reinforced the importance of regulatory and legal considerations in Information Security Management.

Overall, this unit strengthened my awareness of how security standards and disaster recovery planning contribute to organisational resilience. It highlighted the importance of aligning governance frameworks, technical controls, and recovery strategies to reduce the impact of security incidents and ensure business continuity.

Following Rolfe et al. (2001), I reflected on what I learned, why it was important, and how it can be applied in future practice. This helped me connect theoretical concepts of disaster recovery and standards with their practical implementation in real-world organisations.
